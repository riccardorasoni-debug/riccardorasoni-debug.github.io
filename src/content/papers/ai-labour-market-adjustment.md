description: 'Evidence on whether occupations more exposed to generative AI experienced different wage and employment paths after 2022, using UK labour-market data and difference-in-differences designs.'
abstract: >-
  Fornaro and Wolf (2026) show that AI-driven automation can redistribute income from labour to capital and, because workers have a higher marginal propensity to consume, compress aggregate demand: an “AI slump.” This paper asks whether UK occupations with greater ex ante exposure to generative AI experienced differential changes in realised wages and employment after ChatGPT’s November 2022 release. Using official UK earnings and employment data (2016–2025), continuous and categorical exposure designs, and a diagnostic sequence combining conventional and synthetic difference-in-differences, doubly robust DiD, joint pre-trend and placebo tests, and Honest-DiD sensitivity analysis, we find no stable exposure-graded decline in wages or employed-person headcounts; a positive employee-jobs signal survives in one closely related specification family, but its post-2022 timing remains uncertain. These findings concern an occupational labour-market margin and do not directly identify aggregate demand, consumption, or labour-share redistribution.
