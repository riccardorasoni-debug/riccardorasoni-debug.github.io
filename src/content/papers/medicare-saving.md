abstract: >-
  How does public health insurance affect households’ ability to save out of temporary income gains? This paper studies the effect of Medicare eligibility on households’ marginal responses to income shocks using microdata from the Survey of Consumer Expectations. Exploiting the sharp eligibility threshold at age 65 in a regression discontinuity design, the analysis shows that Medicare eligibility increases the saving response to positive income shocks by approximately 8 percentage points. By contrast, responses to negative income shocks change little at eligibility. Auxiliary evidence from the Health and Retirement Study indicates that Medicare substantially reduces exposure to large out-of-pocket medical expenditures, particularly in the upper tail of the expenditure distribution. The findings suggest that public health insurance affects household financial behavior not only through direct expenditure reductions, but also by changing households’ ability to carry forward transitory income gains through saving.
