abstract: >-
  Repeated interviewing is useful for measuring belief dynamics, but it may also change the answers that household surveys elicit. This paper compares panel conditioning across three major expectation surveys: the ECB Consumer Expectations Survey (CES), the Bundesbank Online Panel–Households (BOP-HH), and the New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations (SCE). I harmonize point forecasts, subjective density forecasts, uncertainty, internal consistency, and ex-post accuracy and estimate exact-tenure profiles over the first twelve interviews. Repeated participation systematically reduces subjective uncertainty and point–density inconsistency, but the magnitude and shape of these changes are not survey-invariant. The SCE exhibits the largest declines in point expectations, inconsistency, and forecast errors; BOP-HH is often closer to CES Germany in belief levels and forecast errors, but closer to the SCE in uncertainty. Predicted initial conditions explain an important share of several cross-survey gaps, while substantial tenure dynamics remain after the first interviews. The BOP-HH participation design provides additional mechanism evidence: scheduled breaks partially reverse the decline in uncertainty, whereas randomly assigned prior exposure to the same item has little effect conditional on general survey tenure. A randomized wording experiment produces large contemporaneous shifts in reported beliefs and reference objects, but these effects largely disappear in subsequent interviews. Taken together, the results indicate that measured panel conditioning reflects a combination of initial conditions, survey familiarity, and elicitation design rather than a single survey-invariant learning process.
